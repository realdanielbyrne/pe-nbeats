\section{Trunk Capacity: The Compression Was One Integer in a Constructor}\label{sec:trunk}

The isolation in this section was demanded by three reviewers and the area chair, and it resolves
against the submitted claim. Every comparison below is at a matched parameter budget.

\paragraph{The confound.} The submitted comparison varied the basis and the trunk width while
naming only the basis. A Fourier-style Seasonality block defaults to a trunk of width
$\U = 2048$; every wavelet block defaults to $512$. Matched on width, the two backbones differ
by 1.1\% in parameters. The headline compression attributed to the basis was therefore, in its
entirety, one integer in a constructor.

\paragraph{The width sweep.} We swept trunk width alone on M4-Yearly and M4-Monthly: 160 runs,
ten seeds per rung, five rungs spanning a 48.8-fold parameter range
(Table~\ref{tab:trunk_width}). On Yearly the parameter count falls from 64,645,150 to 1,323,550
while \smape{} moves from 13.645 to 13.707. The five rung means span 0.098 \smape, which is
less than the 0.15 to 0.25 seed spread inside any single rung; Kruskal--Wallis over the five
rungs gives $p = 0.776$ on Yearly and $p = 0.773$ on Monthly. This is a statement about
magnitude, not a bare failure to reject: any accuracy effect of trunk width is bounded below
seed noise across the entire range.

\paragraph{The basis at matched budget.} The $2 \times 2$ crossing of \{Fourier, wavelet\}
against \{uniform trunk, autoencoder trunk\} answers the matched-budget question directly. At
trunk width 128, Fourier scores 13.707 at 1,323,550 parameters and wavelet scores 13.611 at
1,309,470; the Fourier default at width 2048 scores 13.645 at 64,645,150. At the roughly 1M
budget the two bases are indistinguishable, as they are at every matched rung: six comparisons,
no raw $p$ below 0.34. The autoencoder row of this crossing at that budget is not run, and we
disclose the gap plainly rather than leave it ambiguous; the accompanying sweep of the
autoencoder taper, discussed below, is likewise unrun.

\paragraph{The gate and the bottleneck.} The learned latent gate of
Section~\ref{sec:method} is null across twelve comparisons, with minimum $p = 0.24$. One
qualification survives: the ungated Daily arm carries a heavy tail that the gated arm lacks, so
the gate may buy stability rather than accuracy. A gate without a bottleneck cannot be built,
because the gate is defined on the bottleneck values; that cell of the grid does not exist. The
autoencoder bottleneck itself buys no accuracy at matched budget: 13.655 against a Generic
baseline at 13.564. Finally, the half-width taper --- the $\U/2$ intermediate layer of the
bottleneck trunk --- was inherited from the initial implementation and was never justified;
sweeping it is a code change that we disclose as unrun.

\paragraph{The finding.} The interpretable N-BEATS configuration carries roughly fifty times
more trunk capacity than its single-model accuracy requires. This went unnoticed because trunk
width had never been swept. The scope is exact: single models, on the interpretable
configuration, on Yearly and Monthly; whether the published 180-model ensembles exploit the
excess capacity is untested. The claim here is not a better block --- it is a measurement about
N-BEATS.

If the trunk is nearly free, the remaining question is what the frozen basis contributes.
Sections~\ref{sec:completeness}--\ref{sec:trainability} answer it.
