\section{Results on the Accuracy--Size Frontier}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Per-period leaderboards}
\label{sec:results:m4}

Every comparison in this section is a single model against our own
single-model reruns of the \citet{oreshkin2020nbeats} baselines under
identical protocol, schedule, seeds and codebase. The published M4
figures are 180-model ensembles and are never quoted beside a
single-model number; that mismatch is how the submitted version acquired
a headline it could not defend. The reference reruns score 13.564 on
Yearly, 13.307 on Monthly, 6.974 on Weekly, 3.062 on Daily, and 8.906 on
Hourly.

Table~\ref{tab:main_leaderboard} reports the per-period winners under
the paper-sample protocol, and Table~\ref{tab:m4_yearly_full} through
Table~\ref{tab:m4_hourly_full} give the full top-five lists per period
in the appendix. Four disclosures travel with those tables rather than
waiting in an appendix. First, the historic Yearly, Weekly, Monthly and
Daily winning cells carried wavelet family names whose forecast bases
were in fact the identity matrix at those horizons; the accuracy numbers
stand because real wavelets score where the identity scores, but the
labels do not, and the double dagger marks every such cell with the
explanation given in the leaderboard caption itself. Second, the Hourly
entry of the earlier draft was the best of sixteen configurations in its
study and does not reproduce; an independent rebuild lands at 8.885
against the quoted 8.751, so it is replaced by a pre-specified arm. The
rank-tiered frozen-basis arms reach 8.766 with the Karhunen--Lo\`eve
basis and 8.788 with the discrete cosine basis against the 8.758
baseline, indistinguishable from each other (spread 0.022,
$p = 0.770$); the gain there is the tiering form, not a better basis.
Third, Quarterly and Hourly are won outright by the N-BEATS-I+G
baseline, which stays on the leaderboard as such. Fourth, the marked
Hourly baseline row uses the forecast-head activation, a repository
extension rather than a paper-faithful configuration.

\subsection{The frontier claim}
\label{sec:results:frontier}

The claim is the shape of the accuracy-against-size curve, not accuracy.
Table~\ref{tab:sub1m_frontier} states it once and precisely, two rows
per period: the most accurate frozen-basis arm, and the smallest arm
within 0.05 sMAPE of its baseline. On the accuracy rows there is not one
significant loss on any M4 period; the single significant difference is
Weekly, where the compact arm wins by 0.222 sMAPE at $p = 0.038$. On the
minimal rows every period sits within 0.048 sMAPE of its baseline at
15 to 74 times fewer parameters, with one significant difference, Daily
at $+0.048$, $p = 0.031$, reported for completeness rather than as a
finding. Parameter counts include the frozen basis matrices, and the
largest ratio anywhere in the paper is 51$\times$. Compression and
accuracy genuinely trade: Hourly buys parity at 2.4$\times$ or
$+0.165$ sMAPE at 51$\times$, and Daily buys $-0.026$ at 10$\times$ or
$+0.048$ at 74$\times$. Traffic-96 is a loss, 29.37 against 26.24 at
1.81M against 52.3M parameters, and it is printed in the same table as
the wins; burying it would make every other row read as advocacy.

\subsection{Stability and ablations}
\label{sec:results:stability}

The negative results are as important as the winners. Overparameterized
generic stacks are unstable: the 30-stack paper baseline is bimodal on
Quarterly ($15.40 \pm 9.97$), Weekly ($8.78 \pm 4.61$), and Daily
($10.21 \pm 12.01$) under the paper-sample protocol even though its best
seeds look competitive. The failure mode is now measured rather than
hypothesized. Diverged runs place negative forecasts on positive series,
sMAPE scores any such point at its ceiling of exactly 200 whatever the
magnitude, diverged runs put 7.1--7.8\% of their points below zero, and
the fraction scoring 200 equals the fraction below zero to every digit.
Deleting exactly those points recovers the converged score to within
0.11 sMAPE, and rectifying the output removes the failure mode by
construction on non-negative targets, cutting collapses from 107 of
23{,}786 runs to 0 of 136. Rectification destroys cancellation between
signed corrections on sub-band designs, so it is an architecture-scoped
rule rather than a universal one. A paired trend branch repairs the same
conditioning failure without adding expressive power: at complete rank
the discrete cosine arm goes from 28.748 to 9.111 and the
Karhunen--Lo\`eve arm from 27.607 to 9.291, in both cases from 0 of 10
seeds converging to 10 of 10 at $p = 0.00018$, with a no-trend control
still at 0 of 10.

The component ablations resolve cleanly and mostly against the submitted
story. Trunk width is inert across a 48.8-fold parameter range, with
Kruskal--Wallis $p = 0.776$ on Yearly and $p = 0.773$ on Monthly over
160 runs; matched on width, wavelet against Fourier basis is null at
every rung with no raw $p$ below 0.34. The learned latent gate is null
across twelve comparisons, smallest $p = 0.24$, though the ungated Daily
arm carries a heavy tail the gated one lacks, so the gate may buy
stability rather than accuracy. The AE bottleneck buys no accuracy at
matched budget, 13.655 at 8.31M parameters against a generic baseline at
13.564 and 8.22M. Rank tiering beats offset tiering by 0.138 sMAPE at
$p = 0.037$ for an ordered basis while changing nothing for a scrambled
one ($p = 0.695$); the claim that tiering helps does not survive, and
what predicts anything is horizon length and band structure. The
forecast-head activation eliminates the stuck-mode failure entirely
wherever it was tested, including the small-data regime where 63--64\%
of generic-stack seeds stalled; its evidence table is
Table~\ref{tab:active_g_ablation}, the tiering evidence is
Table~\ref{tab:tiered_per_period}, and the scheduler matched pairs are
in Table~\ref{tab:lr_matched}.

\subsection{Transfer to the NHiTS backbone}
\label{sec:results:nhits}

A scoped transfer question survives from the submitted draft: do the
block-level effects survive a change of backbone? Curated subsets of the
block library were rerun on NHiTS \citep{challu2023nhits}, which wraps
the same block interface in multi-rate input pooling and hierarchical
forecast interpolation, on Weather-96 and Traffic at horizons
$\{96, 192, 336, 720\}$ under MSE loss, 100 epochs, and eight seeds, with
the published pooling schedules unchanged. The answer divides cleanly.
The block interface transfers: frozen-basis-plus-trend blocks lead at
the short horizon where the lookback-to-horizon ratio is largest, and
AE-trunk bottleneck variants lead at long horizons of 336 and 720. The
hyperparameters do not transfer: the activation that is decisive on
M4-Hourly is neutral to negative here, and the optimal stack pattern,
bottleneck width, and scheduler all stay dataset- and architecture
dependent. One apparent winner involving a variational trunk is treated
as NHiTS-specific and is not carried into any recommendation. The scope
is honest and narrow: one short-epoch training regime, eight seeds, and
high per-cell variance, so the Traffic-horizon detail stays in the
appendix with that caveat attached rather than in a leaderboard.

\subsection{Out-of-distribution evaluation}
\label{sec:results:ood}

The findings condense to one compact configuration fixed before the
test: ten stacks, a uniform-128 trunk, a frozen complete-rank forecast
basis of any family, a truncated backcast basis, a rectified output
head, and a paired trend branch. On Tourism, over 240 runs across the
three horizons, it lands within the registered margin of 0.5 sMAPE of
the better baseline on all three horizons at 14 to 15 times fewer
parameters, with zero collapses. The narrow trunk holds out of sample,
beating width 512 on all three horizons, significantly on Quarterly at
$-0.622$, uncorrected $p = 0.031$. The same test demoted the trend branch
to conditional against our registered prediction: it costs 0.344 sMAPE
on Quarterly ($p = 0.021$) and 0.609 on Monthly ($p = 0.0073$), and
earns its keep only at the shortest horizon, where it cuts the seed
standard deviation at $H = 4$ from 8.12 to 0.46. Protocol-local winners
are in Table~\ref{tab:tourism_winners}.

One result from outside the registered sweeps must be placed honestly.
An early targeted successive-halving study on Tourism-Yearly, run before
the sweep design of Section~\ref{sec:setup} was fixed, produced a best
configuration scoring 20.864 sMAPE, below the in-sweep Yearly winner.
We report it here as a prior exploratory finding and nothing more: it
was selected after the fact from a larger search under a different
protocol, so it appears in no table, enters no comparison, and carries
no significance statement.

On Weather-96 an alternating frozen-basis-plus-trend stack with an AE-8
trunk reaches $0.138 \pm 0.027$ MSE at 7.1M parameters against 0.183
for the N-BEATS-I+G baseline. On Milk the small-data version of the
stability pattern reproduces: the generic baselines diverge at high
rates while the AE-trunk variants converge reliably, and the 10-stack
winner scores $1.512 \pm 0.572$ against a 1.785 baseline reference.
Traffic-96 remains the outright loss already printed in
Table~\ref{tab:sub1m_frontier}; whether the narrow-trunk default has a
boundary there is stated as open in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Cross-period generalists}
\label{sec:results:generalists}

When one configuration must run across all six M4 periods, two carry the
best mean per-period ranks, in Tables~\ref{tab:generalist_paper}
and~\ref{tab:generalist_sliding}. Under the paper-sample protocol an
alternating frozen-basis-plus-trend pair with tiered offsets, ten
stacks, averages rank 13.33 of 108 and places top-11 on every period at
5--6M parameters. Under the sliding-window protocol the corresponding
leader is the same family of alternating stacks with a doubled backcast
basis rank, mean rank 12.7 of 112 at 16.25M parameters. Where
reproducing \citet{oreshkin2020nbeats} or guaranteed convergence matters
more than rank, the 30-stack interpretable baseline is the alternative
of record, mean rank 16.0 of 68 at 38--44M parameters, with zero
divergence observed. Both generalist tables keep their historical ranks
with a footnote: those ranks were computed within pools that still
contained wavelet-family duplicates and identity-basis cells, so they
understate no competitor and are not recomputed under the collapsed
taxonomy.
