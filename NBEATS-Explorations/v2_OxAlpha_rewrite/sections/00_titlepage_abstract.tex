\begin{abstract}
N-BEATS reaches its M4 accuracy with twenty to forty five million parameters,
a capacity never questioned. Which parts of a basis expansion block carry the
accuracy, and which are inert? I factor each block's unconstrained synthesis
head through a frozen orthonormal basis, leaving two integers, the backcast
and forecast ranks, plus one trunk choice, varying each at matched budgets.

The parameters are in the trunk and are not needed: narrowing it from
2048 units to 128 cuts the count 48.8 fold, $64{,}645{,}150$ to
$1{,}323{,}550$, for 0.098 sMAPE at $p = 0.78$. The compression credited to
the wavelet basis is the trunk, one integer in the original architecture.
Basis identity cannot matter at the rank every reported configuration uses,
where the basis is square and orthogonal and the projection feeding it
absorbs any rotation exactly; thirty six paired comparisons over wavelet,
cosine and random bases are null, minimum $p = 0.084$. Below full rank it
matters greatly, and captured energy, the share of target energy the
retained rows span, reproduces the trained ordering without inversion on two
datasets, rank correlation $-1.0$ on Traffic-96. Trainability is a separate
axis: effective rank tracks convergence at $\rho = +0.949$, bases
concentrating their energy in one direction fail on every seed, and
rectifying the output or pairing a trend branch restores full convergence
without adding expressive power. Instability is negative forecasts on
positive series, which sMAPE scores at its 200 ceiling regardless of
magnitude; deleting those points recovers the converged score to within
0.11, and rectification cuts collapses from 107 of 23,786 runs to 0 of 136.
Out of sample on Tourism the compact configuration matches or beats both
paper baselines at 14 to 15 times fewer parameters, and never collapses in
240 runs.

The gain is position on the accuracy against size frontier, not accuracy:
two significant losses on M4 and an outright loss on Traffic-96, 29.37 sMAPE
at 1.81M against 26.24 at 52.3M.
\end{abstract}
