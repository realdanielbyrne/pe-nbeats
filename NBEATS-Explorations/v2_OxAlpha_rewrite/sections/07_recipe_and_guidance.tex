\section{A Practitioner's Recipe}
\label{sec:recipe}

Everything measured in this paper reduces to a short procedure for
configuring an N-BEATS model on a new forecasting task. The two integers
are the forecast rank and the backcast rank; the trunk choice is its
width. This section states the defaults, when to deviate from them, and
what never helped.

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.975\linewidth}{%
\textbf{Quick pick --- N-BEATS-Slim.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep 1pt
\item Ten stacks, one block per stack, weights shared within stacks.
\item Uniform trunk width 128.
\item Frozen \emph{complete-rank} discrete cosine basis on the forecast
  head ($d_f = H$); truncated backcast basis at $H$ or $2H$ rows.
\item Rectified output head whenever the target is non-negative.
\item Trend branch only if the effective rank of the target window is
  low or the window mean dominates; otherwise omit it.
\item Trend stacks before basis-only stacks.
\item Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$; plateau schedule on Quarterly,
  Monthly and Daily, fixed milestones elsewhere
  (Table~\ref{tab:lr_per_period}).
\end{itemize}
On M4 this preset sits within 0.05 sMAPE of its baseline per period at
15 to 74 times fewer parameters, and it held out of sample on Tourism
within the registered margin at 14 to 15 times fewer parameters with
zero collapses.
}}

\paragraph{Choosing the basis.}
At complete forecast rank the basis identity is provably inert, so do
not tune it. Use the discrete cosine basis by default, for one
operational reason rather than an accuracy reason: it is defined at
every horizon and cannot silently degenerate. A discrete wavelet
transform asked for a target shorter than its filter can act on returns
a valid orthonormal basis whose every row has support one, which is the
identity matrix wearing a wavelet's name; several headline cells of the
submitted version were exactly that. If the basis must be something else,
any complete orthonormal choice synthesizes the same forecasts.

\paragraph{Choosing the ranks.}
Set the forecast rank to the horizon unless parameters must be cut. When
truncating, captured energy --- the share of target energy the retained
rows span --- predicts trained accuracy without inversion, so compute it
before training. The law is exact where it has been tested. At
one-third forecast rank on M4-Hourly, four bases at byte-identical
parameter counts order by capture with no inversion: Karhunen--Lo\`eve
$10.054 \pm 0.130$, discrete cosine $10.287 \pm 0.097$, band-ordered
Daubechies~3 $10.580 \pm 0.102$, and the same wavelet under
singular-value row ordering at 73.946, every adjacent pair at
$p = 0.0078$. On M4-Hourly, sweeping the retained fraction from 0.125
to 1.000 moves sMAPE monotonically from 106.81 to 9.31 at $p = 0.002$
per rung, and on Traffic-96 five bases at identical parameter counts
order exactly by capture (Spearman $\rho = -1.0$, exact $p = 0.017$ at
$n = 5$; run level $\rho = -0.98$ over 25 runs, $p = 9.0 \times
10^{-18}$). The backcast rank is far less sensitive; $H$ or $2H$ rows
against a $3$--$5H$ lookback is standard. Check effective rank next: it
predicts trainability at $\rho = +0.949$, and bases concentrating their
energy in one direction fail on every seed regardless of captured
energy.

\paragraph{Choosing the width.}
Use 128. Width is inert across a 48.8-fold range (Kruskal--Wallis
$p = 0.776$ and $p = 0.773$), so spend capacity on stacks rather than
units. An AE-trunk bottleneck buys no accuracy at matched budget; choose
it only where its small latent layer serves a parameter budget or a
storage constraint, and then prefer a latent dimension of 16 over 32.

\paragraph{The trend branch is conditional.}
Treat the polynomial trend branch as insurance bought where targets are
concentrated, not as a default. On Tourism it significantly costs 0.344
sMAPE on Quarterly and 0.609 on Monthly against our registered
prediction that it was free; it earns its keep only at very short
horizons, where it cuts the seed spread at $H = 4$ from 8.12 to 0.46.
Low effective rank or a dominant window mean are the signals that
justify switching it on.

\paragraph{Tiering, if any.}
If basis rows are distributed across stacks, tier by rank, taking the
leading rows in every stack. Rank tiering lifts mean per-block captured
energy from 0.200 to 1.000 for an ordered basis and beats offset tiering
by 0.138 sMAPE at $p = 0.037$; offset tiering discards leading rows and
only survives where row order is unaligned with energy.

\paragraph{Pre-flight check.}
Both predictive quantities are computable from the basis matrices and
the training data alone. The repository ships a pre-flight utility that
reports captured energy and effective rank for a proposed configuration
before any training run, so a truncation that will not train is caught
at config time; we propose exposing the preset above as a single
constructor profile.

\paragraph{Drop list.}
The following repeatedly failed or bought nothing at matched budget and
should not be re-derived from scratch: the bottleneck-generic family;
pure generic autoencoder stacks; the variational family, which was
catastrophic on M4 at five to ten times the baseline error; skip
connections across a distance of five stacks; and per-period basis-family
shortlists beyond the defaults, since no family produced a per-period win
that survives identity marking. Comparisons across the auxiliary
backcast-loss flag and across normalization regimes are excluded from
this paper's evidence entirely and should be excluded from yours.
