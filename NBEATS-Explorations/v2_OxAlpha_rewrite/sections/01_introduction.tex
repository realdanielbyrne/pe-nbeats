\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Basis expansion made N-BEATS interpretable and accurate on M4
\citep{oreshkin2020nbeats, makridakis2020m4}, and it spawned a family of
architectures that wire fixed transforms --- most often wavelets
\citep{mallat1989wavelet, daubechies1992ten} --- into deep forecasters, among
them wavelet--N-BEATS pairings \citep{pramanick2024fusion}, multiresolution
wavelet networks \citep{deznabi2023multiwave}, wavelet Transformers
\citep{sasal2022wtransformers}, and wavelet-derivative schemes
\citep{zhou2025wavets}. The natural reading of that literature, and the
premise of this paper's submitted version, is that the choice of basis is
where the accuracy lives. This paper started there and tested it. The tests
overturned the premise, and the rewrite reports what replaced it.

\subsection{Thesis}

The rewritten claim is:

\begin{quote}
N-BEATS carries roughly fifty times more trunk capacity than its
single-model accuracy requires. Replacing each block's unconstrained linear
synthesis head with a frozen orthonormal basis makes the rank an explicit
knob and removes the head's ability to emit values outside the target's
range. Which basis fills that slot is provably irrelevant once it is
complete. Two quantities computable before training --- captured energy and
effective rank --- say how accurate a truncated basis will be and whether it
will train at all.
\end{quote}

Everything that follows serves one of four load-bearing results.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The trunk result.} Trunk width is inert across a 48.8-fold
  parameter range: over 160 runs with ten seeds per rung on M4-Yearly and
  M4-Monthly, the five rung means span 0.098 sMAPE against a within-rung
  seed spread of 0.15--0.25 (Kruskal--Wallis $p = 0.776$ Yearly,
  $p = 0.773$ Monthly), from $64{,}645{,}150$ parameters at width 2048 to
  $1{,}323{,}550$ at width 128. The compression the submitted version
  credited to the wavelet basis was one integer in a constructor: the
  comparison had varied trunk width and basis together while naming only the
  basis. The equivalence is bounded rather than merely unrejected.
\item \textbf{The completeness result.} At complete forecast rank with
  linear synthesis, two orthonormal bases differ by a rotation and the
  freely learned projection absorbs that rotation exactly; we verify the
  absorption numerically at residual $5.6\times 10^{-15}$ and measure it by
  swapping Daubechies~3, a discrete cosine basis and random orthonormal
  draws at identical parameter counts --- 36 paired comparisons over six
  periods at ten seeds, none significant, minimum $p = 0.084$. Basis
  identity cannot change what a complete block can synthesize.
\item \textbf{Two pre-training predictors.} Where the basis is truncated,
  captured energy orders trained accuracy exactly: on Traffic-96 five bases
  at identical $3{,}532{,}800$ parameters fall in capture from 0.9714 to
  0.3035 while sMAPE rises from 29.78 to 120.98 without a single inversion
  (basis-level Spearman $-1.0$, exact $p = 0.017$, the floor attainable at
  $n = 5$; run-level $\rho = -0.98$ over 25 runs, $p = 9.0\times 10^{-18}$).
  Trainability is governed separately by effective rank (participation
  ratio): bases concentrating their energy in one direction (1.05, 1.54)
  fail on every seed under a plain linear head where diffuse bases (17.86,
  17.12) never fail, and effective rank tracks convergence at
  $\rho = +0.949$. Neither quantity is wavelet family.
\item \textbf{A measured stability mechanism.} The model's rare divergence
  is negative forecasts on positive series, which sMAPE scores at its
  ceiling of 200 regardless of magnitude; deleting exactly those points
  recovers converged scores to within 0.11 sMAPE. Rectifying the output
  removes the mode by construction on non-negative targets (collapses 107 of
  23,786 runs to 0 of 136), and pairing a trend branch repairs the
  conditioning failure a low-effective-rank basis causes (DCT and
  Karhunen--Lo\`eve arms from 0 of 10 seeds to 10 of 10, $p = 0.00018$),
  though rectification destroys cancellation on sub-band designs.
\item \textbf{Frontier positioning instead of accuracy claims, stated
  against our own reruns.} The gain a compact frozen-basis configuration
  buys is position on the accuracy-against-size curve
  (Table~\ref{tab:sub1m_frontier}), not accuracy; on Traffic-96 the
  baselines win outright and that loss is printed in the main table rather
  than an appendix. All comparisons are single models under identical
  protocol, schedule, seeds and codebase --- the published M4 figures are
  ensembles and are never quoted beside them.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope conditions of the completeness result}

The proposition that basis identity is inert at complete rank carries four
scope conditions, stated here because every use of the result downstream
respects them:

\begin{enumerate}
\item It fixes what the forecast head can \emph{represent}, not what
  training \emph{finds}: expressiveness, not trainability. Trainability is
  the separate effective-rank axis above.
\item The paired trend branch is present in every arm of the identity grid;
  the null is a trend-branch-present result, since the branch absorbs the
  dominant component that otherwise breaks conditioning.
\item It covers the forecast head only. The shipped backcast bases are
  truncated to $H$ or $2H$ rows against a backcast of $3$--$5H$, so they
  live in the truncated regime where families span different subspaces.
\item Tiered configurations truncate the forecast basis as well and
  therefore belong to the truncated regime, where the measurement, not the
  proof, carries the equivalence.
\end{enumerate}

Below full rank the rotation lapses and identity matters before any training
run: captured energy and effective rank take over, which is why the frozen
basis earns its place in the title as method --- it makes rank an explicit
knob, and the two predictive quantities exist only because it does.

\subsection{Position on the parameter-efficiency frontier}

Table~\ref{tab:sub1m_frontier} states the frontier claim once and precisely:
for each period, the most accurate frozen-basis arm and the smallest arm
within 0.05 sMAPE of its baseline. On the accuracy row not one significant
loss appears on any M4 period, and the single significant difference favours
the compact arm (Weekly, $-0.222$, $p = 0.038$); on the minimal row every
period sits within 0.048 sMAPE of its baseline at 15 to 74 times fewer
parameters. Compression and accuracy genuinely trade off --- Hourly buys
parity at $2.4\times$ or $+0.165$ sMAPE at $51\times$ --- and the shape of
that curve, not any single cell, is the result.

\subsection{This paper corrects its own prior claims}

The post-submission experiments contradicted claims the submitted version
made, including claims no reviewer raised. Rather than let corrected numbers
and uncorrected ones coexist, the rewrite carries every correction in a
single ledger (Table~\ref{tab:corrections_ledger}): thirteen entries, nine
of them unraised by reviewers, each stating the submitted claim and what
replaced it. Two further self-disclosures function as standing prohibitions
rather than corrections --- no \texttt{best\_val\_loss} comparison across
\texttt{sum\_losses} settings is valid, and no metric comparison across
normalization regimes is valid --- and no table or sentence in this paper
makes either. Registered predictions that failed are reported as failures.

\subsection{Withdrawal note: the Transformer study}

The submitted version also reported transferring the blocks into Transformer
language models, claiming that a Llama-3.2-1B variant with one bottlenecked
MLP block beats its unmodified base on perplexity (18.81 vs.\ 19.45). We
withdraw that claim and the entire Transformer/Llama material. The 19.45
reference is the stock checkpoint evaluated zero-shot on data it never saw,
while 18.81 follows three epochs of in-domain distillation: the comparison
measures distillation, not architecture, and fine-tuning alone moves
perplexity from 18.899 to roughly 15.4. The companion from-scratch figure of
24.4 is a calibrated extrapolation sitting above its own 19.82 reference,
no frozen-basis arm beat the dense baseline there (19.699 vs.\ 18.539), and
the compute-matched distillation control and unstructured-bottleneck control
were never run. This material leaves the paper entirely and becomes a
separate study with its own controls; nothing else in this paper depends on
it. The small scoped transfer study on the NHiTS backbone
\citep{challu2023nhits} remains, and is reported with the same
matched-budget discipline as everything else.
