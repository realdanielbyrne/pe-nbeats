\section{Full Per-Period Tables and Out-of-Distribution Detail}
\label{app:fulltables}

\subsection{M4 full per-period leaderboards}
\label{app:fulltables:m4}

Tables~\ref{tab:m4_yearly_full}--\ref{tab:m4_hourly_full} list the full
top-5 leaderboard for every (period, protocol) cell summarized in the main
leaderboard of Section~\ref{sec:results:m4}. Numbers are taken verbatim
from the meta-leaderboard report regenerated by the script named in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol:repro}, and the strict health filter of
Section~\ref{sec:setup:stats} is applied to every cell. Within each period,
the paper-sample table mixes step-paper and plateau schedules --- the
per-cell scheduler is shown in its LR column --- while the sliding table
uses cosine-warmup throughout. Absolute sMAPE is never compared across the
two sampling protocols.

Three reading rules apply to every table in this appendix. First,
configuration strings are rendered per the variant taxonomy of
Section~\ref{sec:method_variants}: each row names a variant, its stack
count, ranks, trunk, layout, offsets, and schedule, never an encoded name;
the historical run identifiers that key these rows to their CSVs ship with
the released per-run files. Second, where a top-5 contains rows identical
up to basis family (including identity-equivalent rows), only the
best-scoring representative is kept and footnoted ``further family variants
fall within the null band (Fisher $p = 0.125$)''; no printed score, standard
deviation, or rank is recomputed or pooled. Third, the markers follow the
unified legend of Section~\ref{sec:method}: $\dagger$ for the forecast-head
activation extension, superscript g for a historically gated AE trunk,
$\ddagger$ for a frozen basis that degenerated to the identity matrix at
that family and horizon --- with the disclosure scoped exactly as
Section~\ref{sec:method_identity} specifies, including no $\ddagger$ on any
Hourly cell.

\subsection{Cross-period generalist ranks}
\label{app:fulltables:generalists}

Tables~\ref{tab:generalist_paper} and~\ref{tab:generalist_sliding} report
the top-5 cross-period generalists by mean rank within the 108-configuration
paper-sample pool and the 112-configuration sliding pool respectively.
Both tables keep their historical ranks unchanged, with the pool footnote
required by Section~\ref{sec:results:generalists}: the ranks were computed
before the family collapse, over pools containing family duplicates and
identity-basis cells, so they understate no competitor and are deliberately
not recomputed under the collapsed taxonomy.

\subsection{Out-of-distribution detail}
\label{app:fulltables:ood}

\paragraph{Tourism.} Table~\ref{tab:tourism_winners} lists the
protocol-local winners observed under the comprehensive-sweep protocol on
the three Tourism horizons ($H \in \{4, 8, 24\}$, column-oriented layout,
1{,}311 series, sMAPE loss). The registered compact-preset validation behind
Section~\ref{sec:results:ood} is the stronger evidence and is reported in
full there: the preset was fixed before the test against a registered
margin of 0.5 sMAPE relative to the better baseline on each horizon, met
the margin on all three horizons at 14--15$\times$ fewer parameters with
zero collapses across 240 runs, narrowed the trunk from 512 to 128 on all
three horizons (Quarterly $-0.622$, uncorrected $p = 0.031$), and demoted
its own paired trend branch against the registered prediction --- removing
it costs 0.344 sMAPE on Quarterly ($p = 0.021$) and 0.609 on Monthly
($p = 0.0073$), while at the shortest horizon it cuts the seed standard
deviation from 8.12 to 0.46. The exploratory successive-halving study of
Appendix~\ref{app:configs:ood}, whose best configuration sits below the
in-sweep Yearly winner, remains a prior finding outside every table
(Section~\ref{sec:results:ood}). Per-run evidence:
\texttt{tourism\_preset\_validation\_results.csv} and the comprehensive
Tourism sweep CSV released under \texttt{experiments/results/tourism/}.

\paragraph{Milk.} The univariate Milk production series is the small-data
stress test (one series, $H = 6$, $L = 5H$, sMAPE loss). The ten-stack
winner is an alternating gated-AE trend-plus-frozen-basis pair at sMAPE
$1.512 \pm 0.572$, 1.0M parameters, ten surviving seeds, against an
N-BEATS-I+G ten-stack reference of $1.785$. The sweep reproduces the
small-data version of the generic-stack stability problem of
Section~\ref{sec:results:stability}: untruncated-trunk and generic
baselines diverge at high rates, while AE-trunk variants cut divergence to
the low single digits. The convergence-rate breakdown is released as
\texttt{milk\_convergence\_10stack\_results.csv}; the activation
intervention evidence on the same dataset is in
Table~\ref{tab:active_g_ablation}.

\paragraph{Traffic-96.} Traffic-96 remains the outright frontier loss
printed in the main tables (29.37 sMAPE at 1.81M parameters against 26.24
for the wide baseline at 52.3M): the paper's claim on Traffic is a position
on the accuracy--size curve, not a win. The capture-law evidence behind
Section~\ref{sec:method_predictors} is stated here in its canonical form:
basis-level Spearman $\rho = -1.0$ with exact $p = 0.017$, the smallest
attainable at $n = 5$ bases, and a run-level correlation of $\rho = -0.98$
over 25 runs at $p = 9.0 \times 10^{-18}$. Whether the narrow-trunk default
has a boundary on Traffic-96 is stated as open in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}; the earlier attempt to settle it was
abandoned for seed instability and is not cited as evidence either way.
Per-run evidence: \texttt{traffic96\_basis\_completeness\_results.csv},
\texttt{traffic96\_truncated\_identity\_results.csv}, and the asymmetric-
basis study CSVs under \texttt{experiments/results/traffic/}.
