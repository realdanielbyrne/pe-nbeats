\section{Hyperparameters and Empirical Defaults}
\label{app:hyperparams}

This appendix is the single reference for every hyperparameter exposed by
the model classes used in this paper. For each hyperparameter we report
(i) the constructor default in the released package, (ii) the value used in
the M4 sweeps of Section~\ref{sec:results}, and (iii) the swept range when
applicable. Recommendations are M4-only; out-of-distribution deltas are
summarized in Sections~\ref{sec:setup:data} and~\ref{sec:results:ood}.
Every constructor keyword below is named exactly once here and mapped to
the symbol it instantiates in Section~\ref{sec:method}; elsewhere in the
paper only symbols appear.

\paragraph{Lookback and forecast multiplier.} For all M4 cells we use
$L = 5H$. The paper-sample protocol additionally clamps the valid sampling
window to the trailing $L_h \cdot H$ observations, with $L_h = 1.5$ on
Yearly and Quarterly and $L_h = 10$ elsewhere
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol:faithful}).

\subsection{Training-protocol hyperparameters (sliding window)}
\label{app:hyperparams:sliding}

The two paper-sample variants are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}. The sliding-window protocol used by the
comprehensive sweep trains epoch-by-epoch with the sMAPE loss and Adam at
$10^{-3}$, batches drawn by sliding the lookback over each series; at most
75 epochs with a minimum of 10, early-stop patience 20 epochs and a minimum
improvement of $10^{-3}$, and a cosine schedule with a 15-epoch linear
warmup decaying to a floor of $10^{-6}$. Sub-epoch validation is not needed
under sliding because each epoch's validation pass evaluates the natural
held-out tail of every series.

\subsubsection{Basis family per period: disposition under the family null}
\label{app:hyperparams:basisfamily}

An earlier draft carried a ``best basis family per period'' table. That
table is withdrawn, for two reasons now argued in the main text. First, at
complete forecast rank with linear synthesis, basis identity is inert:
36 paired comparisons across families at complete rank produced no
significant difference (smallest $p = 0.084$), and the cross-dataset
``wavelet family matters'' claim was retracted (Fisher combined
$p = 0.125$ over eight datasets; Table~\ref{tab:corrections_ledger}).
Second, part of that table compared identity-basis cells against each
other --- several historical family labels on M4 degenerated to the
identity matrix at those horizons, so ``which family wins'' was partly
identity versus identity (Section~\ref{sec:method_identity}).

What survives here is narrow and stated for completeness of the record.
(i) The historical sweep used a four-family shortlist plus one further
family that was tested and dropped because it produced no per-period leader
under either protocol; neither fact carries an accuracy claim.
(ii) On Hourly every swept family was genuine, and the family null holds
there on its own. (iii) Per-period recommendations in this appendix are
therefore statements about variant, rank, trunk, layout, and schedule only;
the family that filled each frozen basis is provenance in the released CSVs
and is disclosed cell-by-cell through the identity marker where applicable.

\subsubsection{Block-level rules}
\label{app:hyperparams:block}

Each rule below summarizes a matched-seed finding from the sweep CSVs;
the ablation evidence lives in Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}.

\paragraph{Basis ranks ($d_b$, $d_f$).} Use rank $H$ (the horizon-matched
default) on both paths. At convergence the spread among the tested rank
labels is below 0.014 sMAPE on M4-Yearly across fourteen historical
families and three datasets; the apparent advantage of larger labels early
in training is a convergence-speed artifact. Avoid the half-horizon
forecast rank for $H \leq 8$: no run with it reached the converged regime
on M4-Yearly in the AE-trunk sweep. Unified-layout blocks are more
rank-sensitive (spread 0.37 sMAPE), so the horizon-matched default matters
most there.

\paragraph{AE latent width ($d$).} Where a gated AE trunk is used, $d = 16$
is the default ($n \geq 600$ runs, $p < 0.02$ against $32$ on both
M4-Yearly and Tourism-Yearly). For the ungated AE trunk, $d \in \{5, 8\}$
are statistically equivalent (Mann--Whitney $p = 0.71$, $n = 330$ each),
and $d = 2$ is catastrophically underparameterized
($p < 3 \times 10^{-20}$ against $\{5, 8\}$).

\paragraph{Trend polynomial order ($p$).} Use $p = 3$ for $H \leq 10$ and
$p = 5$ for $H \geq 50$. Significant on Tourism-Yearly ($H = 4$,
$p = 0.008$, favoring 3) and on Weather-96 ($H = 96$, $p = 0.042$,
favoring 5) at convergence. The mid-horizon periods (Monthly $H = 18$,
Weekly $H = 13$, Daily $H = 14$) default to 3 on insufficient evidence. An
earlier ``always 3'' finding was a 10-epoch convergence-speed artifact on
Weather-96 and reversed at 50 epochs.

\paragraph{Trend backbone hierarchy.} On M4, for alternating trend-plus-
frozen-basis stacks, the ordering is untruncated trunk $>$ gated AE trunk
$>$ plain AE trunk. The plain AE bottleneck homogenizes the basis signal in
alternating configurations ($\eta^2 = 0.003$, not significant) and is not
viable there; the learned gate restores competitiveness. On Weather-96 and
Milk the hierarchy reverses (plain AE $>$ gated AE, Mann--Whitney
$p = 0.036$ on Weather); those datasets are out of scope for this appendix.

\paragraph{Stack ordering.} Always place polynomial trend stacks before
frozen-basis, generic, or seasonality stacks. In the ordering study
(M4-Yearly and Weather-96) every significantly different pair favored
trend-first --- including one Bonferroni-significant pair on M4
(Cohen's $r = 0.83$) --- and no pair favored the reverse. Treat this as a
fixed convention, not a hyperparameter to search.

\paragraph{Unified versus alternating layout.} Under the paper-sample
protocol on M4, alternating single-branch stacks beat the unified layout by
roughly ten mean-rank points (14.1 vs.\ 24.3 across the 53 curated
configurations over six periods). Default to alternating whenever the
larger parameter budget is acceptable; reserve the unified layout, or its
AE-trunk forms, for parameter-constrained scenarios. The plateau scheduler
flipped the Monthly winner to the unified layout at thirty stacks
($13.240$); on every other period alternating is at least co-optimal.

\paragraph{Forecast-head activation ($\dagger$).} Off (the constructor
default) is the robust choice on M4 and the paper-faithful setting.
Switched on for the forecast head only, it helps on Hourly --- every paper
backbone gains 0.07--0.15 sMAPE there --- and ties or loses on every other
period (Appendix~\ref{app:ablations:activeg}). It is an extension beyond
\citet{oreshkin2020nbeats}: paper-faithful claims exclude $\dagger$ cells.

\paragraph{Not exercised on M4.} Cross-stack residual injection
(\texttt{skip\_distance}, named once here per the API-name convention)
never helped on M4 in the configurations tested and slightly hurt deep
stacks. The auxiliary backcast-reconstruction loss term defaults to off and
is reserved for a dedicated convergence study; no result in this paper
compares across it.

\subsection{Excluded configurations}
\label{app:hyperparams:droplist}

The configuration classes below were excluded \emph{a priori} from the
curated paper-sample subset and from every headline leaderboard; each is
supported by at least one released CSV. Encoded names are replaced by
descriptions, per the taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:method_variants}.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep -1pt
\item \textbf{Bottleneck-generic blocks}: universally worst on M4.
\item \textbf{Pure variational trunks}: mean sMAPE 55--68 on Quarterly,
      Monthly, Weekly, and Daily, unusable (see also
      Appendix~\ref{app:ablations:doesnotwork}).
\item \textbf{Thirty-stack generic baseline on Quarterly, Weekly, Monthly}:
      bimodal collapse, sMAPE standard deviation 7.4--9.5.
\item \textbf{Cross-stack residual injection at distance five}: never helps
      on M4.
\item \textbf{One further basis family on M4}: no per-period leader under
      either protocol; dropped from the shortlist without further claims.
\item \textbf{Thirty-stack tiered configurations carrying the forecast-head
      activation}: run-to-run divergence outliers.
\item \textbf{Step-paper schedule on Quarterly, Monthly, Daily}: dominated
      by the plateau scheduler (Table~\ref{tab:lr_per_period}).
\item \textbf{Pure generic stacks with AE or gated-AE trunks}: bottom out
      at mean rank approximately 38.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-period generalists}
\label{app:hyperparams:generalists}

When a single configuration must run across all six M4 periods --- cross-period
reporting, ensemble bases, ablation harnesses --- three configurations
dominate by mean per-period rank, in agreement with
Section~\ref{sec:results:generalists}. Under the paper-sample protocol with
all-six coverage, the leader is an alternating frozen-basis-plus-trend pair
with tiered basis offsets, ten stacks: mean rank $13.33$ of 108,
5.07--6.06M parameters, top-11 on every period. Where reproducing
\citet{oreshkin2020nbeats}, or guaranteed convergence, matters more than
rank, the thirty-stack interpretable baseline is the alternative of record:
mean rank $16.0$ of 68, 38--44M parameters, zero divergence observed. Under
the sliding protocol the leader is the same alternating family at thirty
stacks with a doubled backcast basis rank: mean rank $12.7$ of 112,
16.25M parameters; a smaller alternative with a gated AE trunk reaches
3.66M parameters at mean rank $21.0$.

Both generalist tables in Section~\ref{sec:results:generalists}
(Tables~\ref{tab:generalist_paper} and~\ref{tab:generalist_sliding}) keep
their historical ranks with a footnote: those ranks were computed within
pools that still contained family duplicates and identity-basis cells, so
they understate no competitor and are not recomputed under the collapsed
taxonomy.
