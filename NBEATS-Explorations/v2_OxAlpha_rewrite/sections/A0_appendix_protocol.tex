\section{Training Protocols, Reproducibility, and Compute}
\label{app:protocol}

This appendix documents the two paper-sample training protocols in full,
so that every M4 number in Section~\ref{sec:results} can be reproduced
from a single configuration file (the verbatim protocol block ships in
Appendix~\ref{app:configs}). Both variants share the sampling strategy and
step budget of the original N-BEATS training procedure;
they differ only in the learning-rate scheduler.

\subsection{Faithful replication (MultiStepLR)}
\label{app:protocol:faithful}

The faithful variant (\texttt{comprehensive\_m4\_paper\_sample.yaml}) is a
direct copy of the training procedure described by
\citet[Section~5.2 and Appendix~D]{oreshkin2020nbeats}, with no deviations
except the small validation-cadence correction described below.

\paragraph{Sampling.} Each gradient step draws a batch of 1024
(series, anchor) pairs uniformly at random \emph{with replacement} from the
training pool. A series is eligible if it carries at least $L + H$
observations; for a chosen series of length $T$, the anchor index is
sampled uniformly from $[T - L_h \cdot H,\; T - H]$, restricting valid
windows to the trailing $L_h \cdot H$ observations. Per-period $L_h$ is
fixed to the values of Table~\ref{tab:datasets}: $L_h = 1.5$ on Yearly and
Quarterly, $L_h = 10$ on Monthly, Weekly, Daily, and Hourly. The lookback
length is $L = 5H$.

\paragraph{Optimization.} Adam at $\eta_0 = 10^{-3}$ with
$\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$, and no weight decay. The sMAPE loss is
computed per step on the 1024-window batch. The gradient budget is
$10^{5}$ total steps, with one Lightning epoch defined as 1{,}000 steps
(approximately 100 epochs in total).

\paragraph{Learning-rate schedule.} MultiStepLR with ten evenly spaced
milestones at $\{10\text{k}, 20\text{k}, \ldots, 100\text{k}\}$ steps, the
rate multiplied by $0.5$ at each milestone. The learning rate is therefore
halved at each 10\% boundary of the training budget, which is exactly the
schedule used by \citet{oreshkin2020nbeats}.

\paragraph{Validation and early stopping.} Validation runs every 100
training steps (\texttt{val\_check\_interval}$=100$) on the held-out window
of each series. Early stopping monitors validation sMAPE with patience 15
(counted in val-check units, so at least 1{,}500 steps must elapse before
stopping can fire) and a minimum improvement of $10^{-3}$. The sub-epoch
validation cadence is the only departure from a strict line-by-line copy of
\citet{oreshkin2020nbeats}; without it, early stopping fires on the first
warmup-corrupted check and collapses to \texttt{best\_epoch} $\in \{0,1\}$.

\paragraph{Other settings.} One block per stack, weights shared within each
stack, ReLU activations throughout, forecast multiplier 5, and the default
block widths (uniform trunk width $U = 512$; seasonality width 2048;
polynomial width 256).

\subsection{Plateau variant (ReduceLROnPlateau)}
\label{app:protocol:plateau}

The plateau variant (\texttt{comprehensive\_m4\_paper\_sample\_plateau.yaml})
is identical to Appendix~\ref{app:protocol:faithful} in every respect except
the learning-rate schedule. The motivation is that MultiStepLR commits to a
fixed decay schedule independent of how training is actually progressing; a
plateau scheduler can react to validation stalls and find a more aggressive
effective decay on cells whose loss surface is well behaved.

\paragraph{Learning-rate schedule.} \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} with factor
$0.5$, patience 3 (val-check units, so the rate is reduced after three
consecutive 100-step val checks without an improvement of at least
$10^{-3}$), cooldown 1 (one val check skipped after each reduction before
monitoring resumes), minimum learning rate $10^{-6}$, mode \texttt{min},
monitored on validation loss. The scheduler steps on the same cadence as
validation (every 100 training steps), so its trigger logic operates in the
same time units as early stopping.

\paragraph{Effect.} Empirically the plateau scheduler delivers more rate
reductions than MultiStepLR on M4 Quarterly, Monthly, and Daily, and fewer
on Yearly and Weekly, matching the observed differences in the per-period
leaderboards (Section~\ref{sec:results}). All other settings --- sampling,
$L_h$, batch size, step budget, sub-epoch validation, early stopping,
optimizer, loss, block widths, weight sharing, seed budget --- are inherited
unchanged from Appendix~\ref{app:protocol:faithful}.

\subsection{Why two paper-sample protocols}
\label{app:protocol:why}

The N-BEATS paper reports a single training procedure, so any one-protocol
comparison conflates the architectural change with whatever artifacts that
specific schedule introduces on a new architecture. By running each
architecture under both the faithful protocol (MultiStepLR) and the plateau
variant (ReduceLROnPlateau) we can attribute an observed gain to one of
three categories: (i) gains under both protocols are robust evidence for
the architectural claim; (ii) gains under the faithful protocol only
constitute a head-to-head replacement for the published baseline; (iii)
gains under the plateau protocol only are a finding contingent on a more
responsive schedule, which we report as such rather than as a clean
architectural win. This control is the reason every architectural claim in
Section~\ref{sec:results} survives a schedule-confound check
(Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocols}); the per-period scheduler verdicts that
fall out of it are tabulated in Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams}.

\subsection{Compute resources}
\label{app:protocol:compute}

All experiments reported in this paper ran on a single workstation: two
NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs (32\,GB GDDR7 each), an AMD Ryzen Threadripper 7970X
(32 cores / 64 threads), 128\,GB RAM, CUDA 13.0, driver 580.126.09. Each
individual run uses one GPU; the two GPUs executed independent jobs in
parallel from a shared work queue. The M4 paper-sample budget is fixed at
$10^{5}$ gradient steps with batch size 1024
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol:faithful}); sliding-window M4 runs use at most
75 epochs with patience-20 early stopping
(Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams:sliding}). The comprehensive sweep
(112 configurations $\times$ 6 M4 periods $\times$ 10 seeds) together with
the curated paper-sample ($53 \times 6 \times 10$) and plateau
($53 \times 6 \times 10$) reruns dominates the budget; the post-submission
studies enumerated in Section~\ref{sec:setup:sweeps} add a comparable share,
and the Tourism, Traffic, Weather, and Milk sweeps a smaller fraction.
