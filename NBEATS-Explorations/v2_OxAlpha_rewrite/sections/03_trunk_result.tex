\section{The Trunk Result: Where the Parameters Were}
\label{sec:trunk}

The submitted compression comparison varied two things and named one. It
varied the synthesis basis, which the paper discussed, and the trunk width
feeding it, which it never mentioned: a Seasonality block defaults to a
trunk width $U = 2048$ while every wavelet block defaults to $U = 512$.
Matched on trunk width the two backbones differ by 1.1\% in parameters, so
the entire headline compression was one integer in a constructor. This
section isolates width alone, under the protocols and statistics of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

Five rungs, $U \in \{2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128\}$, were swept on M4-Yearly
and M4-Monthly in the interpretable trend-plus-seasonality configuration,
ten random seeds per rung, 160 runs, no divergences:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Trunk width $U$ & 2048 & 1024 & 512 & 256 & 128 \\
Parameters & 64{,}645{,}150 & 17{,}111{,}070 & 5{,}140{,}510 & 2{,}104{,}350 & 1{,}323{,}550 \\
sMAPE (Yearly) & 13.645 & 13.609 & 13.671 & 13.680 & 13.707 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The result is a bounded equivalence, not a bare failure to reject. Across
a 48.8-fold parameter range the five rung means span 0.098 sMAPE --- less
than the 0.15--0.25 seed standard deviation inside any single rung --- and
Kruskal--Wallis over the rungs gives $p = 0.776$ on Yearly and $p = 0.773$
on Monthly. The differences across the whole range are therefore bounded
below the noise floor of one cell: shrinking the trunk from 64.6M to
1.32M parameters moves accuracy by less than a typical seed moves it
inside any one configuration.

With width held fixed, the remaining components separate just as cleanly.
Matched on width, the wavelet-versus-Fourier basis comparison is null at
every rung, with no raw $p$ below 0.34 across the six comparisons; at the
narrowest rung Fourier scores 13.707 at 1{,}323{,}550 parameters against
13.611 for the wavelet at 1{,}309{,}470. This discharges the RootBlock row
of the requested $2\times2$ crossing \{\emph{Fourier}, \emph{wavelet}\}
$\times$ \{\emph{uniform}, \emph{AE}\} at the roughly 1M budget, on
M4-Yearly plus a longer-horizon period. The learned latent gate is null
across twelve paired comparisons (smallest $p = 0.24$); a gate without a
bottleneck cannot be built, because the gate is defined on the bottleneck
values, so that cell of the grid cannot exist. The autoencoder bottleneck
buys no accuracy at matched budget either: 13.655 at 8.31M parameters
against a generic baseline at 13.564 and 8.22M.

Three conclusions survive. First, the parameter reduction is attributable
to the trunk, not to the frozen basis, the bottleneck, or the gate; this
reattribution is carried in the corrections ledger
(Table~\ref{tab:corrections_ledger}). Second, the interpretable N-BEATS
configuration carries roughly fifty times more trunk capacity than its
single-model accuracy requires, and the finding went unnoticed because
trunk width had never been swept. Third, the narrow trunk is not merely
harmless: it survives out of sample on Tourism, where width 128 beats
width 512 on all three horizons, significantly on Quarterly at $-0.622$
($p = 0.031$, uncorrected); the frontier consequences are consolidated in
Table~\ref{tab:sub1m_frontier} and Section~\ref{sec:results}. Two scope
conditions bound the claim: it is a single-model result, and it is
measured on Yearly and Monthly. The published M4 figures are 180-model
ensembles, and whether ensembling exploits the excess capacity is
untested.

\paragraph{Disclosed as not run.}
Four committed follow-ups had not landed before this revision was
completed, and each is disclosed here plainly rather than left as an
ambiguous gap.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The AE row of the $2\times2$ crossing.} Committed in
response to the request for the full \{\emph{Fourier}, \emph{wavelet}\}
$\times$ \{\emph{uniform}, \emph{AE}\} crossing at the ${\sim}1$M budget;
it is not run. The AE-bottleneck null at matched budget above is the
adjacent evidence and predicts that the row will not change the verdict,
but the cell itself remains empty.
\item \textbf{The $U/2$ taper sweep.} The hourglass taper
$U \rightarrow U/2 \rightarrow d \rightarrow U/2 \rightarrow U$ was
inherited from the initial implementation and never questioned; sweeping
the intermediate width at fixed latent width $d$ is a code change plus
training, and it is not run. Two adjacent measurements suggest it will
not rescue accuracy --- the bottleneck buys none at matched budget, and
the gate on top of it is null --- but the sweep will be reported however
it falls.
\item \textbf{The weight-decay arm.} Of the four candidate explanations
for the parity (inductive bias, regularisation, parameter count,
protocol), regularisation is the one without an arm at fixed parameters;
it is not run. Inductive bias is eliminated by the completeness result of
Section~\ref{sec:completeness}, parameter count by this section, and the
protocol is guarded by the scheduler control of Section~\ref{sec:setup}.
\item \textbf{The Traffic trunk-width rerun.} Whether the narrow-trunk
default has a boundary on Traffic-96 is open. A first attempt was
abandoned as invalid: a configuration error of ours (a missing
learning-rate schedule, early-stopping patience 10 against the reference
sweep's 20, and weight sharing disabled) inflated the seed standard
deviation to 5.43 against the reference sweep's 0.50, leaving the attempt
underpowered by roughly $10\times$. Its five completed runs are invalid
and are cited nowhere in this paper. The rerun, using the reference
sweep's schedule, patience, and weight sharing, is queued; until it
lands, Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states the boundary as open.
\end{enumerate}
